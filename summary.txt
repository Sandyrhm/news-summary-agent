World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. Two days of US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine ended in Abu Dhabi with no ceasefire or deal on territory, but the sides agreed a large prisoner swap that US envoy Jared Kushner called the biggest release of the war; Kushner and Witkoff next go to Moscow, then Dublin (Reuters). Iran said the Strait of Hormuz would 'never return' to its pre-war status and sent seven conditions for peace through Qatar, while US prosecutors charged three men over nearly 20 alleged Iran-backed plots, including one at RAF Fairford (Al Jazeera; BBC). The COP31 leaders' summit opened in Antalya, attended by 102 leaders; UN chief Antonio Guterres warned the slow climate response had put the world 'on a path to destruction' (AFP). In Washington, the House voted 211-204 to send articles impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, killing the effort in this Congress, and Democrat Scott Colom defeated Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi (Reuters; AP). Israel's 26th Knesset was sworn in with no government: Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc holds 53 of 120 seats and Gadi Eisenkot's 48, and Eisenkot has 28 days to form one (Reuters). Israeli airstrikes killed three Palestinians, including two brothers, in Gaza City, medics said (Reuters). Sudan's army said it shot down two drones over West Kordofan and East Darfur, its claimed tally now 29 (Anadolu Agency). El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele said Salvadoran troops and Haitian police destroyed the headquarters of the 400 Mawozo gang near Port-au-Prince; reports of up to 20 dead are unconfirmed (BBC). US October inflation, up 0.4% on the month against a 0.3% forecast, hit Wall Street and Asian markets, and Nvidia dropped more than 5% (Reuters). Cases in Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000, government data showed (Reuters). A Florida judge let Donald Trump's $10bn BBC defamation case proceed, dismissing his unfair-trade claim (Reuters).
